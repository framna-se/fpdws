Workshop 2

The room is the user

We can't bring real users into the room, so the room becomes the users.

30 minutesPairs, role play

The product

Parkinator 2000

Parkinator 2000 is a parking app. You pay for street parking from your phone, start and stop a session, and get a receipt.

The brief

To the interviewers
You are on the team building Parkinator 2000. Management wants to add a loyalty program: park ten times, get the eleventh free. Your job is to find out whether that is the right thing to build. You have eight minutes with a real user. Go.

How it runs

Pair up. One of you plays a user from a persona card, the other interviews. Decide between you who starts as which. Halfway through, you swap roles.

If you hold a card

You are the user. Open your card, read it, and keep it to yourself. Play it as written: answer what you are asked, and do not volunteer what the card tells you to hold back.

  • Card A: Tomas, delivery driver
  • Card B: Nadia, consultant
  • Card C: Jonas, parent of two

If you are interviewing

You are on the Parkinator 2000 team. You have eight minutes with a real user to find out whether the loyalty program is the right thing to build. Do not open the cards.

What to do

  1. Pair up. Decide who holds a card first and who interviews. The cardholder picks a card.
  2. Round 1, 8 minutes. We will call time.
  3. Swap roles. New card, new interviewer. Round 2, 8 minutes.
  4. Write it down. In your notebook: the one thing that surprised you.

Interviewing and stuck?

Open this

Ask about the last time, not the future

  • "Would you use this?" invites a guess about an imagined future. "Walk me through the last time you parked" gives you something real to dig into.
  • Then: What triggered it? What did you do first? Where did you get stuck?
  • Get the whole trip, start to finish, not just the moment they open an app.

Follow the effort

  • What took the most time? What have you already tried?
  • What happened the last time this went wrong?
  • What happens if you leave it undone?

Treat complaints as clues

  • "It's expensive" or "it's annoying" is a starting point, not an answer. Ask what they did about it last time.
  • Give them room. Silence is fine. Let them finish before you ask the next thing.

Hold your idea back

  • If you describe the loyalty program and ask whether they like it, they will say yes. That tells you nothing.
  • If you must mention it, do it in the last minute, after you have heard what actually happened.
