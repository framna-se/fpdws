Workshop 1

The brief

Management has a feature request. Should you build it, or build something else, or maybe not build anything at all?

45 minutes

The brief

You are the engineering team. This arrived this morning.

From
Head of Sales
To
Engineering
Subject
Onboarding assistant before Q4

We lost two of our three biggest deals last quarter. In both debriefs the buyer brought up onboarding, and in one of them the competitor's AI setup assistant came up by name.

We need an AI assistant in the app that walks new users through setup step by step, so this stops being an objection. It has to be live before Q4 so we can show it in the renewal conversations.

Can I get a plan and a rough estimate by Friday?

The four questions

  1. Who pays for this, and why?
  2. What would have to be true for this to create value?List the assumptions.
  3. What is management actually asking?The request is a proposed answer. What was the question?
  4. What is the cheapest way to find out?

What to do at your table

You have about 25 minutes; we will call time. You are not here to plan or design the thing. If someone starts drawing architecture, go back to "What is management actually asking?" 😃

  1. 90 seconds individually. In your notebook, write down every answer you can think of to these two questions. Keep each one short.
    • Who pays for this?
    • What would have to be true for it to create value?
  2. Pick a scribe, then go round the table. Each person reads one answer aloud. Keep going until nobody has anything new. The scribe keeps the table's list in their notebook.
  3. Answer the four questions together. Work through them as a table. The scribe writes down the answers.
    • Who pays for this, and why? Settle it from what came up in the round.
    • What would have to be true for this to create value? Take the table's list from the round, group it into assumptions, and number them. This is the list you vote on in the next step.
    • What is management actually asking? The request is a proposed answer. Agree on the question behind it.
    • What is the cheapest way to find out? Discuss it.
  4. Vote for the riskiest assumption. The scribe reads the numbered assumptions aloud. Each person names one: the assumption that, if it turned out to be wrong, would sink the whole idea, and that nobody has actually checked. Most votes wins. On a tie, argue for one minute and pick.
  5. Write one sentence. Use this template: "We shouldn't build X until we know Y."
    • X is the feature the brief asks for.
    • Y is the assumption that won the vote, rewritten as the thing you would need to find out before building.
    For example: "We shouldn't build automatic weekly reports until we know where the time preparing them actually goes." When the time is up, you will be given further instructions on what to do with the sentence.
